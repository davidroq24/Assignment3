2. Design Rationale

Why I Chose Matter.js

For this assignment, I wanted to build a playful browser mini-game without relying on heavy 3D tools like Three.js or A-Frame that we already explored in class. Writing realistic 2D collisions, angular rotation, and multi-sided polygon physics from scratch in vanilla JavaScript requires complex vector math, so I used Matter.js, a dedicated 2D rigid-body physics plugin.

This proof-of-concept highlights three specific advantages of the library:

  • Polygon & Circle Collisions: Using Matter.Bodies.polygon() with six sides, the grey moon rocks tumble, interlock, and roll naturally when struck by circular yellow projectiles.
  • Custom Cartoon Rendering Loop: By stepping the physics world with Matter.Engine.update() inside a standard HTML5 canvas loop, I was able to layer a cartoon astronaut, Earth in the background sky, and bold comic outlines on top of the physics bodies.
  • Dynamic Gravity Controls: Adjusting engine.gravity.y through UI buttons lets players immediately feel the difference between low lunar gravity, Earth gravity, and near-weightless deep space.

Opportunities Beyond the Proof-of-Concept

While this prototype focuses on a single lunar sandbox stage, the same foundation could expand into a complete web application:

  • Ammo Limits & Stage Progression: Tracking knocked-off rocks via Matter.js body coordinates would enable a three-star scoring system, limited energy orbs per round, and multi-stage planetary levels.
  • Web Audio Sound Effects & Particle Trails: Pairing Matter.js collisionStart events with synthesized retro sound effects and dust bursts would make impacts feel much more tactile.
  • Interactive Science Education: A themed interface like this could help middle-school science students experiment with projectile velocity and planetary gravity in an approachable, game-like way.
