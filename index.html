Design Rationale

Why I Chose Matter.js

I wanted to build a small playable game for this prototype without using heavy 3D frameworks like Three.js or A-Frame that we already covered in class. Writing polygon collisions, rotation, and bouncing from scratch in plain JavaScript takes a lot of vector math, so I used Matter.js, a lightweight 2D rigid-body physics library.

This demo focuses on three things Matter.js handles cleanly out of the box:

  • Multi-Sided Polygon Physics: Using Matter.Bodies.polygon() with six sides, the grey moon rocks stack, tumble, and roll realistically when hit by the yellow energy orbs.
  • Custom Cartoon Canvas Loop: By updating the physics world with Matter.Engine.update() inside a standard HTML5 canvas loop, I could draw a cartoon astronaut, Earth in the sky, and cratered terrain right over the physics bodies.
  • Live Gravity Switching: Changing engine.gravity.y through the top controls lets players immediately test how low lunar gravity compares to Earth gravity or deep space.

Opportunities Beyond This Prototype

Right now this is a single sandbox stage, but the same setup could easily grow into a fuller web project:

  • Ammo Limits & Star Ratings: Checking how many hexagonal rocks get knocked off the platform would make it easy to add a limited orb count per round and unlock harder moon bases.
  • Sound Effects & Impact Dust: Listening to Matter.js collisionStart events could trigger synthesized retro sound effects and small dust particles whenever a rock hits the ground.
  • Interactive Science Demos: A visual setup like this works well for introductory physics pages where users can experiment with launch angles and planetary gravity hands-on.
